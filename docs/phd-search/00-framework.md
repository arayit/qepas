# Selection Framework

**Revision 3, 1 September 2026.** Replaces rev. 2 entirely.

---

## The filter

Three things. That is the whole framework.

1. **Table-top experimental.** The experiment sits in a room the group controls.
2. **No extreme equipment dependence.** No synchrotron, FEL, accelerator or beamline
   owned by someone else. No PIC / integrated photonics. No PhD where the work is
   mostly cleanroom fabrication.
3. **The PI is intellectually first-rate.** Ömer İlday is the reference point. This is
   the criterion that actually decides, and it is a judgement about the person and the
   science, not about the topic.

Plus the obvious: Europe, start Spring–Fall 2027.

---

## What rev. 1 and rev. 2 got wrong

Both earlier versions filtered on things that are not in the list above, and both
produced false rejections because of it. Recorded here so it does not happen again.

**Topic labels are not a filter.** "Atomic physics", "quantum optics", "quantum
materials", "drifts to chemistry", "precision metrology" — these are descriptions of
subject matter, not of quality or working style. Rev. 1 rejected Rauschenbeutel on one
of these while he is, by any measure, among the strongest people in the survey. Several
other names were lost the same way. **Under rev. 3, no group is rejected for what its
webpage calls itself.**

**Employability is not a filter, and it is not mine to apply.** Rev. 1 and 2 filtered
toward topics with visible job pipelines — mid-IR sensing, source development,
metrology — on the assumption that the thesis topic determines the job. That is not how
it works, and it is not the stated position: general experimental competence is what
transfers. **Employability is not assessed in these documents.** If a group is
table-top, not equipment-bound, and run by someone excellent, it goes on the list, and
the career question is decided separately and by you.

**"Develops its own source" was too narrow.** It is a good sign, not a requirement.
What matters is that the group builds rather than buys the thing that makes its physics
possible — a comb, a laser, a nanofibre, a trap, a microscope. Where a group buys its
lasers but invents its platform, that counts.

**Scoring was false precision.** Rev. 2's weighted 45-point scale made the ranking look
measured when it was a judgement all along. Dropped. Groups are sorted into three bands
and the reasoning is written out in words.

## Bands

- **A — Go.** Passes all three. PI is unambiguously top-tier: founded a subfield, or
  holds ERC Advanced/Synergy-scale funding on their own ideas, or has that standing by
  reputation.
- **B — Real candidate.** Passes all three, PI is strong and independent but not
  field-defining, or is too new to tell.
- **C — Check one thing.** Would pass, but there is a specific factual question
  (how much beamtime? how much cleanroom? is the PI still taking students?) that
  decides it.

Anything failing filter 1 or 2 is simply not listed.

---

## City

City is applied **after** the group list exists, never as a pre-filter. A group in
band A in a small town stays on the list; the city question is then a trade you make
knowingly.

Workable cities, from the earlier survey: Vienna, Berlin, Barcelona, Rotterdam,
Brussels comfortably; Amsterdam, Hamburg, Paris, Milan with one strain each. Small
university towns (Erlangen, Jena, Göttingen, Regensburg, Rostock, Enschede) are not
excluded — they are a cost to weigh against a band-A advisor.

## Verification

Group leadership, retirements and openings change often. Verify before contacting
anyone. Items sourced indirectly are marked **[V]**.
