# PhD Group Search — City-First Phase

**Compiled:** 1 September 2026
**Scope:** Berlin (TU Berlin, HU Berlin, MBI) and Amsterdam (AMOLF, ARCNL, VU, UvA), plus a comparative shortlist of other candidate cities.
**Purpose:** working document for the city-first stage of the PhD search. Group data verified against institutional pages as of Sep 2026; verify again before writing to anyone, since German and Dutch group structures change often.

---

## 0. Selection criteria in force

These are the filters applied throughout this document.

**Hard requirements**

- Experimental work, table-top scale.
- No dependence on large facilities (synchrotron, FEL, accelerator, beamline). Expertise must be portable.
- Intellectual depth comparable to Ömer İlday's work — the calibre of the science is the primary filter, not the topic. "At the end of the day it is all nonlinear optics."
- The group develops its own source rather than running turnkey lasers.
- Europe. Fall 2027 target start; Spring 2027 acceptable.

**Preferred**

- The deliverable is a device, a technique, or device physics — not merely a measurement, and not merely a better light source that someone else then uses.
- Mid-IR or field-resolved spectroscopy adjacency.
- Small, independent, competitively funded group (ERC-scale).
- City large enough to live in alone with a cat, cosmopolitan, good transit.

**Excluded**

- Pure quantum computing hardware; also groups labelled quantum materials / quantum optics.
- Precision atomic/molecular metrology aimed at fundamental physics tests.
- Semiconductor metrology, wafer inspection, electron microscopy, materials characterisation.
- Vacuum/beamline-heavy work (Wörner at ETH was dropped on this basis).
- Groups drifting into chemistry.
- Reconstruction-algorithm development as the core activity.
- Cleanroom-fabrication-dominated or software/simulation-dominated PhDs.

**Deprioritised, not closed:** integrated photonics / PIC and microcombs. Reason: the design–simulation–fabrication–characterisation loop feels industry-facing and thin on fundamental physics. Confirmed second-hand by a PhD student in Del'Haye's group. Not a hard rejection.

### 1.5 Berlin verdict

**Three full matches:**

1. **Nathalie Picqué (MBI, Div. C)** — strongest name in Berlin on this profile. Hänsch lineage, founding-level figure in dual-comb, entirely table-top, develops her own source, output is a method. At or above Ömer's calibre. The metrology here is comb development, not wafer or TEM characterisation, so it does not fall into the excluded metrology category. The division explicitly invites unsolicited applications.
2. **Sven Ramelow (HU)** — mid-IR imaging and spectroscopy with undetected photons is conceptually elegant work; nonlinear frequency conversion is central, table-top, small group, and it maps onto the mid-IR background. Caveat: the group carries a quantum-optics label.
3. **Günter Steinmeyer (MBI C2)** — mid-IR solid-state lasers, OPO/OPA, pulse characterisation, with Petrov's nonlinear crystal work under the same roof. Best Berlin instance of "develops its own source." Below Picqué in calibre and the group is established rather than hungry.

**Borderline:** Woerner (MBI C3), Nagy (MBI A3), Mermillod-Blondin & Engel (MBI 4.3).

**High calibre, wrong axis:** Rauschenbeutel (HU).

**Structural note:** Berlin's optics weight sits in X-ray, attosecond beamlines, cleanrooms and integrated quantum photonics. The intersection of table-top + own source + intellectual depth is genuinely narrow here — three names is not an accident of searching. A city-first strategy centred on Berlin yields fewer options than Erlangen would.

### 2.5 Amsterdam verdict

**Two full matches:**

1. **Ewold Verhagen (AMOLF)** — optomechanics and nonreciprocal photonics; conceptually deep, output is device physics, table-top, no vacuum/beamline, ERC-scale, small group. At Ömer's calibre. Actively recruiting, which is a practical advantage.
2. **Said Rodriguez (AMOLF)** — driven-dissipative nonlinear photonics, bistability, threshold behaviour. Treats nonlinearity at the system level rather than the material level, which is the same move the burst-mode paper makes. Established tenure-track, not junior-junior.

**Half match:** **Paul Planken (ARCNL)** — direct continuation of the photoacoustics work, table-top, but the institute is anchored to ASML metrology and the intellectual claim is more technique-driven than Verhagen's or Rodriguez's.

**Borderline:** Kraus (XUV/vacuum), Witte (reconstruction-heavy), Koenderink (fabrication + integrated photonics), Schreck (cold atoms + vacuum).

**Excluded outright:** the entire VU LaserLaB precision-metrology block; Amitonova and den Boef (metrology/computational imaging); Polman (electron beam); Versolato (plasma + ASML).

**Structural note:** Amsterdam has no group in the "develops its own ultrafast source and does nonlinear optics with it" mould — the gap that Steinmeyer and Picqué fill in Berlin is simply not filled here. Against that, AMOLF's two names are individually sharper than most of the Berlin set: AMOLF positions itself as a fundamental physics institute and selects its group leaders on tenure track, which is institutionally the closest thing to Ömer's definition of intellectual work.

**Practical:** AMOLF and ARCNL PhDs are NWO employees on four-year contracts with full employee status — this fits the "PhD as a job, not a continuation of education" framing even better than German TVöD positions. Against that, the Amsterdam rental market is one of the tightest in Europe, which bears directly on the self-contained-apartment-with-cat requirement.

## 3. City comparison

What makes Berlin and Amsterdam work: large dense population, daily life functioning in English, a visible subculture (music, nightlife, art), a non-car-dependent urban fabric, and a social structure that is open to outsiders. Few European cities hit all five.

### Closest matches

| City | Life fit | Science on-axis | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Barcelona** | Highest cultural match on the list; huge international population, street life denser than Berlin's | ICFO — largest photonics concentration in Europe; attosecond, ultrafast, nonlinear optics and nanophotonics under one roof | ICFO is in Castelldefels, 30–40 min by train, not in the city. Rents have hardened; salaries well below Northern Europe |
| **Vienna** | Large, multicultural, big Turkish community, excellent transit, rents unusually moderate for a Western capital because of the municipal housing system | TU Wien Photonics Institute — Baltuška already on the target list, live referral channel through Ömer | Probably the easiest large city for the self-contained-apartment-with-cat criterion. More orderly and less edgy than Berlin |
| **Brussels** | Most international city in Europe, multilingual, cheap for a capital, rough-edged in a Berlin-like way, large Turkish community | Moderate in-city (VUB/ULB B-PHOT, applied photonics) | Ghent is 35 min by train, Leuven 25. Living in Brussels and commuting to Ghent is a real option if Ghent is revisited |
| **Rotterdam** | Most diverse and least polished Dutch city; Amsterdam's energy without the polish, markedly cheaper, large Turkish population | Delft 25 min by train (Errando-Herranz on the list) | The practical answer to wanting the Amsterdam lifestyle without hitting the housing wall |

### Partial matches

- **Paris** — top of the list on cosmopolitanism and very high science density (Institut d'Optique, LOA, C2N, INSP, LKB). Two problems: most labs are on the Saclay plateau, so you live in the city and commute an hour; and PhD pay relative to rent is among the worst in Europe.
- **Hamburg** — the German city closest to Berlin in character: port, music scene, alternative fabric, smaller but wealthier. DESY/CFEL there (Herr already known). Rents above Berlin, below Munich.
- **Copenhagen** — very international, bicycle city, fully liveable in English, but "clean and designed" rather than colourful; socially less open than Berlin and housing runs on queue systems. DTU names (Mørk, Oxenløwe, Hu) already on the list.
- **Milan** — Italy's cosmopolitan end, strong design and nightlife. Cerullo at Politecnico is on-calibre in ultrafast and nonlinear optics; Melloni and Bogoni already on the list. High rents by Italian standards, low pay.
- **Stockholm** — English is unproblematic, very international, but housing is genuinely hard for foreigners and the fabric is orderly and quiet.

### Not matches, despite frequent recommendation

Munich, Zurich, Geneva — money and science, but conservative, expensive and socially closed. Prague and Budapest — cheap and attractive, growing international scene, but the optics investment there is ELI-type large facility, already an excluded category. Lisbon — attractive lifestyle, weak science base on this axis. Leipzig — a cheaper, smaller Berlin; genuinely lively, but weak in optics.

### The core tension

The best scientific matches found so far — Fattahi and Stiller — are in Erlangen, a town of 115,000, with Nuremberg half an hour away. The same holds for Jena, Göttingen, Enschede and Lyngby. In Europe, most groups matching the "table-top + develops its own source + intellectual" profile sit in small university towns; the large cosmopolitan cities have invested either in large facilities (Hamburg, Paris, Prague) or in applied/industry-matched institutes (ARCNL in Amsterdam, Brussels).

Three places genuinely deliver the intersection: **Barcelona (ICFO)**, **Vienna (TU Wien)** and the **Rotterdam–Delft axis**. Berlin also stays in, because MBI is inside the city and has table-top groups.
