### 2.5 The honest objection

The score is high and the gates are clean, so the framework says yes. But the framework
does not know what four years feel like. Two things to weigh that no axis captures:

**What you would actually do.** A PhD in this group is a cold-atom machine PhD.
Magneto-optical traps, magnetic coils, UHV, ECDL locking, a great deal of fibre pulling
and taper characterisation, and quantum-optics theory alongside it. It is superb
training. It is not nonlinear optics, it is not source development, and it is not
mid-IR. The portable expertise you leave with is nanophotonic atom interfaces and
waveguide QED.

**The pivot is real and one-directional.** Going from mid-IR photoacoustics into
waveguide QED is a full change of field. It is entirely doable at PhD entry — that is
what a PhD is for — but afterwards you are a quantum optics person, and the QEPAS and
burst-mode work becomes backstory rather than foundation. The framework deliberately
weights adjacency at ×1 so that this cannot veto a calibre-5 group. That was the right
call. But it should be a decision you make consciously, not one the arithmetic makes
for you.

**Counter-argument worth taking seriously.** The 2024 PRX is about *superradiant bursts*
— collective emission where the ensemble's nonlinearity produces a threshold and a
burst. That is structurally the same move as the burst-mode paper: engineering
nonlinearity at the system level rather than the material level. The topic distance is
large; the intellectual distance is smaller than it looks. This is the same argument
that put Said Rodriguez at AMOLF high on the list.

**Verdict: keep, ranked 2 in Berlin.** Not a rejection, not the top pick. The thing to
resolve before writing is whether you want continuity or a clean pivot — and that
question is the same one for Verhagen and Rodriguez in Amsterdam, so it is worth
settling once.

## 3. New Berlin candidates found in this pass

### 3.1 Ugaitz Elu — FHI, Ultrafast Core-Level Spectroscopy

**The most direct topic continuation available in Berlin.**

Group leader in Martin Wolf's Department of Physical Chemistry at FHI. PhD at ICFO with
Jens Biegert on *high peak power mid-IR OPCPAs for extreme nonlinear photonics*. The
group builds bright, broadband, CEP-stable mid-infrared sources and uses them for
core-level (soft X-ray) spectroscopy of correlated materials.

Recent: *A high-energy self-CEP-stable OPCPA seeder using a high-average-power
picosecond narrowband Yb thin-disc laser*, Opt. Lett. **50**, 6646 (2025); *Frontiers of
Bright CEP-Stable Broadband Infrared Sources*, Laser Photonics Rev. (2026). An FHI
postdoc advert for ultrafast soft X-ray absorption spectroscopy of correlated materials
appears to belong to this group **[V]**.

**Caveat you must resolve.** The soft-X-ray core-level end is the same profile as
Wörner at ETH, who was dropped. Under the revised G2 the distinction is ownership:
table-top HHG in the group's own lab passes, booking a synchrotron does not. Elu's HHG

**Ask directly what the split is** before committing.

### 3.2 Tobias Kampfrath — FU Berlin / FHI, Terahertz Physics

Professor at FU Berlin since 2017, and PI of the THz Physics group at FHI since 2010.
**ERC Advanced Grant "ORBITERA", €2.5M over 5 years (2024)**, on terahertz orbitronics.
He established THz spintronics as a field. The group develops **spintronic THz emitters**
— thin-film metallic stacks that convert femtosecond pulses into broadband THz — which
are a genuine device output, now used well beyond his own lab.

| Axis | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| A ×3 | 4 → 12 | Founding-level within THz spintronics; ERC Advanced. Below Rauschenbeutel and Picqué on breadth of influence |
| B ×2 | 4 → 8 | The spintronic emitter is a device; orbitronics is device physics. Marked down because much of the output is materials spectroscopy |
| C ×2 | 4 → 8 | Emitters, THz setups and detection built in-house; driving lasers commercial |
| D ×1 | 4 → 4 | THz time-domain detection **is** field-resolved detection — the same measurement philosophy as the field-resolved mid-IR line, on a different carrier |
| E ×1 | 3 → 3 | Large, established, full professor with ERC Advanced |
| | **35 / 45** | |

The rev. 1 exclusion list contains "quantum materials", and his FU group is literally
named *Terahertz Physics of Quantum Materials*. Under rev. 2 §3, the question is whether
the material is the object or the medium. Here it is closer to the medium — the physics
is about generating and controlling THz fields and reading out ultrafast currents.
**Include.**

### 3.3 Alexander Paarmann — FHI, Nonlinear Chemical Imaging / infrared nano-optics

Mid-IR and THz nonlinear optics of polar dielectrics: surface phonon polaritons,
second-harmonic generation from lattice anharmonicity, and **wide-field SFG microscopy**
as an infrared super-resolution technique. Mid-IR nonlinear optics where the
nonlinearity comes from the lattice rather than electrons — conceptually unusual and on
the right axis.

**G2 caveat:** part of the work uses the FHI IR/THz free-electron laser (in-house, so it
passes the ownership test) and the near-field endstation at FELBE, HZDR (external — that
part fails). The table-top OPA-based SFG microscopy is the part that passes cleanly.

Score: A 3 (×3=9), B 4 (×2=8), C 4 (×2=8), D 4 (×1=4), E 3 (×1=3) → **32 / 45**.

## 5. What this does to the structural conclusion

Rev. 1 concluded: *"Berlin's optics weight sits in X-ray, attosecond beamlines,
cleanrooms and integrated quantum photonics. Three names is not an accident."*

That was an artefact of surveying only MBI, TU and HU. With FHI and FU included and the
topic-label rejections reversed, Berlin yields **one group above the relocate-anywhere
threshold and a cluster of five between 32 and 36** — comparable to, and arguably
deeper than, Amsterdam's two-plus-one.

The revised structural claim: Berlin's problem is not scarcity, it is that its on-axis
groups are **scattered across five institutions** (MBI, HU, FU, FHI, TU) with no single
place to apply. That is an application-logistics problem, not a scientific one, and it
argues for treating Berlin as a strong Tier-1 city rather than a thin one.
